Cfilms Real Estate Adviser — the complete setup

An always-on AI adviser that answers out loud, runs your booking pipeline, sorts your cards, and keeps the admin off your plate. This version supersedes the 17 August plan: the core is built and running, setup is a single command, and the monthly cost has come down a long way.

01 — What changed since the first plan

The core is built, and it costs nothing to run

The August plan estimated $25–100/month. Two decisions removed almost all of that.

The first plan assumed CREA would pay a per-word voice service and a per-token AI service. Neither is necessary. The voice runs entirely on your own machine — no account, no API key, no per-word cost — and the thinking is routed through a free model router that picks a no-cost model for each request. Both were benchmarked on real hardware, not estimated.

What that means practically: the numbers below are measured on a 2020 M1 MacBook with 8 GB of memory — deliberately the worst machine this will ever run on. Your Mac Mini will be faster.

What is already working

This is not a mockup. The following runs today and can be demonstrated:

  • CREA speaks aloud in a natural voice, generated on-device — and says its own name properly. There are 26 voices to choose from, all free.
  • It wakes when you say “Hey CREA”, heard through a real microphone, with nothing transmitted until you do. It can learn your voice and answer only you.
  • It holds your entire job pipeline in an Obsidian vault and answers questions about it — "what have I got on this week, and how much am I owed?" returns the right shoot, the right client, and the right dollar figure, spoken back.
  • The job dashboard builds itself from the vault: pipeline counts, upcoming shoots, outstanding money, and anything stalled in editing.

It is running against realistic test data — invented agents, invented addresses — because none of your real accounts are connected yet. Connecting them happens during setup.

02 — How it fits together

Four layers, one machine

The split from the first plan holds. Here is what actually occupies each layer.

Layer 1

The voice

Listens for "Hey CREA", turns your speech into text and its reply into audio. All on-device — nothing is transmitted until the wake phrase fires.

Layer 2

The brain

Hermes decides what you meant and which skill to run. It also owns the schedule, so the daily briefing and overnight jobs use the same brain.

Layer 3

The hands

n8n does the plumbing to outside services — Acuity, Google, WhatsApp, Drive — as visual workflows rather than code you'd have to maintain.

Layer 4

The memory

An Obsidian vault of plain text files. Every job, client and shoot log is readable and editable by you directly, and portable if you ever drop CREA.

Why n8n carries the integrations

The original plan had every connection written as custom code. That's a maintenance burden nobody wants on someone else's machine. n8n replaces most of it with visual workflows, and it already ships a native Acuity Scheduling trigger — your bookings arrive as an event the moment they're made, with nothing custom to build. Google Calendar, Google Drive and WhatsApp have first-party nodes too, and there are several hundred published WhatsApp booking workflows to start from rather than from scratch.

Why the model router matters

OmniRoute sits between Hermes and the AI models. Hermes asks it for "the fastest good model" and OmniRoute picks one — preferring free providers, falling back automatically when one is rate-limited. You get resilience and a $0 bill without Hermes needing to know or care which model answered. If you later want the highest-quality model for a specific job, it's a one-line change, not a rebuild.

06 — The WhatsApp question

Your number stays your number

This was the biggest risk in the original plan, and it turns out to be avoidable.

The obvious way to read WhatsApp messages is Meta's official Business API. It's also a trap for you specifically. Connecting a number to that API means that number can no longer use the normal WhatsApp app, your local chat history is deleted, and incoming messages can be held for up to a month while the migration completes. For a business whose bookings arrive by WhatsApp, that's not a risk worth taking.

Hermes offers a second path. hermes whatsapp pairs by QR code, exactly like WhatsApp Web on a laptop. Your number stays a normal WhatsApp number, your history stays intact, and CREA reads incoming messages the same way a second device would.

The honest caveat

This method uses an unofficial connection library. Meta's terms don't formally permit non-official clients, and there is a small risk of a number being restricted. It's widely used and rarely a problem in practice, but you should know about it before we switch it on — and if you'd rather not take it, we use a second SIM for CREA and leave your main number untouched.

07 — Call recording

The one part with a legal condition attached

Worth getting right rather than fast. This is the only capability here with criminal exposure rather than commercial.

NSW requires all parties to a private conversation to consent to it being recorded. The Surveillance Devices Act 2007 has a narrow exception for protecting your own lawful interests — but "so I get the booking details right" is convenience, not protection, so that exception isn't available to us. The exception also fails outright where a recording is passed to people who weren't part of the conversation, which is what any transcription pipeline does.

That leaves consent, and consent is workable. In NSW consent can be implied — if a caller is clearly told at the start that the call is recorded and continues talking, that's generally accepted. So CREA is built to play a spoken disclosure before it retains a single second of audio:

# from crea.config.json
"require_disclosure": true,
"disclosure_text": "Just so you know, I record calls
                    so I get the booking details right.",
"retain_audio": false,       # transcribe, then discard the recording
"abort_if_disclosure_fails": true

Two deliberate choices there. retain_audio: false means the recording is transcribed for the booking details and then deleted — CREA keeps the date, time and address, not a library of your clients' voices. And abort_if_disclosure_fails means if the disclosure doesn't play for any reason, nothing is recorded at all.

Before this goes live

I'm not a lawyer and this isn't legal advice. Every other capability here you can switch on at your own discretion; for this one, run the disclosure wording past someone qualified first. It's a cheap conversation and it makes the difference between a feature and a liability.

08 — The card pipeline

Plug in the card, go have a shower

The flagship skill, unchanged in ambition from the first plan — with one safety change.

The sequence is exactly as originally specified: detect the card, copy everything off, split the files into separate shoots by looking for time gaps between shots, upload each shoot to Drive in its own named folder, push it into Higgsfield, and tell Narendra it's ready.

The one change is the last step. The original plan had CREA format the card automatically once everything was copied. It won't, and it ships with that disabled:

# from crea.config.json
"shoot_gap_minutes": 90,     # a longer break = a new shoot
"verify_copies": true,
"format_card": "never"       # never | ask | auto

The reasoning is simple. Until Drive confirms the upload, that card is the only copy of a paid shoot in existence. Every other failure in this system costs you an inconvenience; this one costs you a job, a client, and a reputation. CREA will verify every copy and then tell you the card is safe to format. Once you've watched it get that right a dozen times, we can move it to "ask", and eventually to "auto" if you want. That's your call to make later, not a default to inherit now.

Take the Mac Mini out of the box, plug it in, and go through Apple’s normal first-time setup. Then open the Terminal app (press ⌘ Space, type “terminal”, hit Enter) and paste this one line:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/skw-fuj/crea/main/install.sh | bash

Near the end it stops and asks about three accounts — WhatsApp, Acuity, and Google. Each one is a yes/no, each one is optional, and each one can be done later with crea connect. WhatsApp shows you a QR code to scan with your phone, exactly like linking WhatsApp Web. Acuity asks you to paste a key. Google opens a browser window and you click Allow.

Say no to all three and CREA still installs and still talks to you — you just won't have bookings flowing in yet.

If something goes wrong

It won't fail silently. Every step reports whether it actually did something, was already done, or failed — and at the end it re-checks all five core components rather than assuming they came up. If anything is broken it tells you exactly what, writes a log, and asks you to send it to me. It's also safe to run again as many times as you like; anything already installed is left alone.

What happens the moment it finishes

CREA starts talking. It's already listening for its name, and it restarts itself whenever the Mini reboots — you never start it manually. The last thing the installer prints is the short list of things worth trying first:

# say this out loud
"Hey CREA — what have I got on this week?"

# or type it
crea ask "how much am I owed?"
crea status                 # is everything healthy?
crea connect                # add an account you skipped

Open the Obsidian app and you'll find your job vault sitting there — every shoot, client and note as a plain document you can read and edit yourself. Change something there and CREA knows about it immediately.

Moving machines later

Every setting specific to you — your name, your folders, your keys, your preferences — lives in a single settings file. Moving CREA to a different Mac is copying that one file across. Nothing gets reinstalled or reconfigured by hand.

12 — Talking to it

How to actually use your voice

The installer sets all of this up. This is what you need to know to use it.

It's pronounced KREE-ah

Not "cray". This matters more than it sounds like it should — the speech recognition is tuned for that pronunciation, and CREA says its own name that way too. Say it "cray" and it will usually still catch you, but "kree-ah" is what it's listening for.

macOS will ask for the microphone

The first time CREA listens, macOS pops up a box asking to use the microphone. Say yes. If you miss it or hit Don't Allow, CREA sits there hearing nothing forever — it is the single most common reason a setup like this looks broken when it isn't. You can fix it later under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone.

Just talk

"Hey CREA, what have I got on today?"
"Hey CREA, how much am I owed?"
"Hey CREA, mark the Castle Hill job as shot."

Say the name, wait for the short "Yep?", then say what you want. It listens continuously and everything stays on the machine — no audio goes anywhere until you have said its name, and the recording is thrown away once it has understood you.

If it doesn't hear you

What's happeningWhat to do
No response at allRun crea status. It reports honestly which part is down rather than guessing.
Never wakes upCheck the microphone permission above. Then check the input device is the one you're speaking into — a connected iPhone or a headset can quietly steal it.
Wakes at the wrong timesIt is deliberately tuned to accept near-misses rather than miss you. Tell me and I'll tighten it.
Hears you but answers oddlyExpected early on. It only knows what's in the vault — connecting Acuity and Google is what makes the answers real.

Making it answer only you

Out of the box CREA answers anyone who says its name — the way a HomePod does. If you'd rather it only answered you, it can learn your voice:

crea enrol              # say the phrase a few times
crea voice-check on     # now it only answers you

Enrolment takes about a minute. It asks you to say “Hey CREA” five times, and it wants a bit of variety — closer, further away, mid-sentence — because that makes it far more reliable than five identical recordings. It tells you if one of them came out noticeably different, so a bad enrolment doesn't quietly make it flaky.

Do it on the Mini itself, with the microphone you'll actually use. A voice sounds measurably different through a different mic, so a print recorded elsewhere works less well.

If you'd rather it just answered anyone — handy if your editor or an assistant ever needs to ask it something — leave it off. That's the default.

It errs towards answering

If the voice check can't run for any reason, CREA answers anyway rather than going silent. An assistant that stops responding because a background service fell over is worse than one that occasionally answers someone else.

You never have to use your voice

Everything CREA does by voice it also does by typing, and the job vault is plain documents you can open and edit in Obsidian yourself. Voice is the convenient path, not the only one.

13 — CREA on your phone

Three ways, two of them free

You're out shooting most of the day, so this matters more than it looks. Don't decide on day one — use it for a fortnight first, then pick the one that matches what you actually reach for.

1. Let CREA message you  Free

Once WhatsApp is connected, CREA can message you — the morning brief, tomorrow's shoots, a nudge when a job has been sitting in editing too long. It comes to your normal WhatsApp. No app, no subscription, nothing to set up beyond the connection you've already made.

For "what's my next job and where", this covers most of it. Start here.

2. Talk to it from the car  Free

An iPhone Shortcut that reaches the Mini at home, so you can ask CREA something with your hands on the wheel and hear the answer back. This is phase 7 in the build order.

It uses a private network between your phone and the Mini rather than opening the Mini up to the internet. Nothing is exposed publicly, and there's no port forwarding on your router. That distinction matters: an always-on machine with an open door on a home connection is how people get compromised.

3. The whole vault, on your phone  $4–5/month

Obsidian Sync puts the actual job vault on your iPhone — every shoot, client and note, readable and editable. Change something on the train and CREA picks it up.

Worth being straight: $4/month billed yearly, $5 monthly, on a system otherwise running at $0–15. That's a real percentage increase even if it's small money. Students get 40% off.

Why not just use iCloud, which is free?

Because iCloud removes files from your Mac when it decides they're not being used, leaving a placeholder that downloads on demand. That's invisible when a person opens a note. It is not invisible to CREA, which reads the vault automatically every fifteen minutes — an emptied-out job note is a job CREA can't see.

This isn't theoretical. While building this, two files in an iCloud folder showed as completely empty until they were manually pulled back down.

Obsidian Sync keeps a full copy on every device and never empties one out. For a folder that a background program reads on a schedule, that's the difference that matters — more than the version history you're nominally paying for.

What I'd do

Run it for two weeks on WhatsApp alone. If you find yourself wanting to read and edit the vault on your phone rather than just get answers, that's when Sync earns its money. If you only ever want answers, you never need it.

14 — When things go wrong

The five things most likely to bite

None of these are exotic. They're the ones that catch every always-on setup, so they're handled or flagged rather than left for you to discover.

If this happensWhat's going onWhat to do
CREA stops answering overnight The Mac went to sleep. Handled — CREA keeps it awake by itself, without changing your power settings. Nothing. If it does happen, crea status will say.
Power cut, or you restart the Mini Everything restarts on its own. You never launch anything. Nothing. Give it a minute after boot.
Internet drops Voice and the job vault keep working — they're on the machine. Bookings, Drive and WhatsApp pause and catch up when you're back. Nothing. Nothing is lost.
An account stops working A key expired or was revoked. CREA won't guess or half-run — the skills needing it say exactly what's wrong. crea status, then crea connect for that one.
WhatsApp unlinks itself Linked devices drop off after a long idle period. Normal, not a fault. crea connect whatsapp and scan again.

The one that would actually hurt

Losing a shoot. It's why CREA verifies every file copied off a card, and why it will never format one. It tells you the card is safe and leaves the decision with you. Until Drive confirms every file, that card is the only copy of a paid job in existence — every other failure here costs you an inconvenience, that one costs you a client.

If something's wrong and you can't tell what

Run crea status. It reports what's actually working rather than what's supposed to be, including whether the Mac's clock disagrees with CREA's. Send me what it prints and I'll know where to look.
